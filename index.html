Conservation & Future Exploration

The Space Heritage Charter

Why NASA's abandoned hardware is not "debris", but humanity's most sacred extraterrestrial archaeological sites.

Off-World Archaeological Sites

On Earth, the Pyramids of Giza and Stonehenge are protected cultural treasures. On the Moon and Mars, Apollo 11's Eagle descent stage and Opportunity's resting place are humanity's first footsteps beyond our home world. They tell the story of human curiosity overcoming the laws of gravity.

The Danger of Rocket Plumes

In the vacuum of the Moon, rocket engines during future commercial landings kick up high-speed sandblasts of sharp regolith. Without preservation boundaries (such as NASA's recommended 2-kilometer keep-out zones), historic bootprints and fragile thermal foils could be obliterated by incoming spacecraft.

Priceless Materials Science

When Apollo 12 retrieved Surveyor 3's camera after 31 months on the Moon, engineers learned how solar cosmic rays, extreme thermal cycles (-250°F to +250°F), and micrometeorites pit metals and optics. Abandoned hardware acts as a natural multi-decade cosmic laboratory.

The Artemis Accords & UN Treaties

Under Section 9 of the international Artemis Accords, signee nations commit to preserving outer space heritage. Team smAsh advocates for recognizing these silent pioneers under an international treaty as the First Extraterrestrial Historic Monuments.
